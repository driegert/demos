The Monty Hall problem

Behind three doors are one car and two goats. You pick a door, then the host — who knows exactly where the car is — opens a different door and shows you a goat. Two doors remain closed: yours, and one other. Should you keep your pick, or switch?

Things to think about

  • Before the host does anything, how likely is it that your first pick is the car? Does the host opening a door move the car?
  • The host knows where the car is and never opens it. Think about the rounds where your first pick was a goat: which door is the host forced to leave closed?
  • Each round, Keep and Switch can't both win and can't both lose. Why not? What does that say about the two win counts after many rounds?
  • Ten rounds is a small sample. Try the 100-round button a few times and see how much the win rates wobble.
